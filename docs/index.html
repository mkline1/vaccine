🔬 Per-Contact Vaccine Efficacy Dashboard

A Streamlit simulation tool for back-of-the-envelope per-contact VE calculations in a heterogeneous sexual-partner-count population.

Created: July 23, 2026  ·  Platform: Python / Streamlit

📋 Project Overview

This dashboard was built to explore how per-contact (per-act) vaccine efficacy translates into observable trial-level efficacy (VE = 1 − CIR) when the study population has a heterogeneous distribution of sexual partner counts. The core question it addresses:

If a vaccine reduces per-contact transmission by some amount V, how does that manifest in cumulative incidence — and how sensitive is that estimate to the distribution of sexual-activity risk buckets in each arm?

The tool runs a configurable number of Monte Carlo simulations (default 100) over a 2-year study period divided into four 6-month intervals. Results are summarised with medians and IQRs across runs, and displayed as interactive charts and tables.

👥 Partner Count Distribution

Each person is assigned to one of five activity buckets based on their number of sexual partners per 6-month period. Bucket assignment is drawn once at baseline and remains fixed for all four periods.

Bucket Default Proportion Sampling within bucket
0 – 1 partners 0.01 (1%) Discrete Uniform{0, 1}
2 – 5 partners 0.15 (15%) Discrete Uniform{2, 3, 4, 5}
6 – 10 partners 0.20 (20%) Discrete Uniform{6 … 10}
11 – 50 partners0.50 (50%) Discrete Uniform{11 … 50}
>50 partners 0.10 (10%) Discrete Uniform{lower … upper}; default 51–100; user-configurable
Missing0.04 (4%) Not directly sampled — redistributed (see below)

Key design choices for this bucket

  • The two arms can have completely independent distributions — counts are entered per-arm as integers that must sum to N_vax and N_placebo respectively.
  • Inputs are count-based (integer people, step = ±1) rather than proportions, to make it easy to explore moving one person from one bucket to another.
  • A live proportion table and a running total / validation indicator update immediately as counts are changed; the simulation run button is blocked until both arms sum exactly to their target N.
  • A Reset button per arm re-calculates the default proportions for whatever N is currently set.

Missing data handling (per arm)

Each arm independently offers three strategies for the 4% missing:

  • Redistribute evenly — missing count split equally across the five active buckets (default).
  • All → highest risk (>50) — conservative assumption; people who did not report partner counts had more partners.
  • All → lowest risk (0–1) — optimistic assumption.

Because each arm has its own selector, a "split by group" scenario — e.g., highest-risk in the vaccinated arm vs. redistribute in placebo — is naturally supported without a separate mode.

🦠 Transmission Model

Per-contact infection probability

p_per_contact (placebo) = p_contact_infected × p_transmission p_per_contact (vaccinated) = p_contact_infected × p_transmission × (1 − V)
ParameterSymbolDefaultRange
Probability contact is infected p_ci0.030.00 – 1.00; step 0.01
Probability of transmission given infected contact p_t0.500.00 – 1.00; step 0.05
Per-contact vaccine efficacy V0.000.00 – 1.00; step 0.05

Per-period infection probability

For each person who is still uninfected at the start of a period, the number of partners k is drawn from Discrete Uniform within their fixed bucket. The probability of acquiring at least one infection across those k independent contacts is:

P(infected this period) = 1 − (1 − p_per_contact)^k

A single Bernoulli draw is then made. If the person is infected they exit the at-risk pool permanently and are not exposed in subsequent periods.

Trial-level VE

CIR = CI_vaccinated / CI_placebo VE = 1 − CIR

Because each person is a binary (ever-infected) outcome with no reinfections, this is equivalent to a risk ratio — the standard VE estimator in efficacy trials.

📌 Model Assumptions

#Assumption
11 partner = 1 sexual contact. Partner count is used directly as the number of independent exposure events per period.
2p_contact_infected is fixed and homogeneous. Every contact carries the same probability of being infected, regardless of time, geography, or partner identity.
3Each contact is independent. No network effects, no partnership duration, no repeated contacts with the same partner within a period.
4Bucket assignment is fixed for the entire 2-year study. A person's behavioural risk category does not change across the four periods.
5Partner counts are redrawn independently each period from Discrete Uniform within the person's fixed bucket.
6No reinfections. Once infected a person exits the at-risk pool permanently. VE is estimated from binary (ever-infected) outcomes.
7VE acts multiplicatively on per-contact transmission probability: p_vax = p_ci × p_t × (1 − V).
8No waning immunity. Vaccine efficacy V is constant across all four periods.
9The >50 partners bucket is modelled as Discrete Uniform between user-specified bounds (default 51–100).
10Missing data redistribution is applied once at baseline and treated as fixed for all four periods.

🗂️ Design Decisions Log

Key choices made during the design session and the reasoning behind them.

Platform: Streamlit (Python) vs. R/Shiny?

Chosen: Streamlit Streamlit Community Cloud is free with no active-hours cap, whereas shinyapps.io free tier caps at 25 hours/month. Deployment is faster (push to GitHub → connect to share.streamlit.io → share URL).

How should the >50 bucket be modelled?

Decision Discrete Uniform with user-configurable lower and upper bounds (default 51–100). The upper bound matters substantially for the cumulative incidence in that bucket, so it was made explicit and interactive rather than hidden.

Should the two arms share one distribution or have independent ones?

Decision Independent per-arm distributions. This allows exploration of differential missing-data assumptions and potential imbalance in behavioural risk across arms.

Should distribution inputs be proportions or integer counts?

Decision Integer counts (step = ±1), so the user thinks in terms of "move 1 person from bucket A to bucket B". The UI shows live proportions alongside counts and blocks the run until totals are exactly correct.

What to do with missing-data participants?

Decision Three strategies selectable per arm: (1) redistribute evenly across all five buckets; (2) assign all to highest-risk (>50); (3) assign all to lowest-risk (0–1). Having separate selectors per arm implicitly covers the "split by group" scenario.

Is bucket assignment fixed across periods or redrawn?

Decision Fixed for the full two-year study. Reflects the assumption that a person's behavioural risk category is a stable characteristic, not something that changes period-to-period.

How to handle reinfections?

Decision No reinfections. Once infected a person is removed from the at-risk pool permanently. VE is therefore estimated as 1 − (risk ratio), equivalent to the standard trial estimator, without requiring knowledge of timing of infection events.

Incidence rate ratio vs. cumulative incidence ratio?

Decision CIR (= risk ratio). Because infections are binary and there is no within-period timing information, IRR would require person-time at risk, which is not modelled here. CIR is the correct and simpler estimator for this setup.

Mean or median for summary statistics?

Decision Medians throughout (top-line metrics, histograms, bar charts, summary table). With N = 500 per arm the CIR distribution can be right-skewed in some settings, making median more robust. Mean and SD are also shown in the summary table for completeness.

Terminology: "attack rate" vs. something else?

Decision Replaced "attack rate" with "cumulative incidence" / "proportion infected" throughout — more precise epidemiological language that matches what is actually computed.

How many simulation runs?

Decision Default 100; user can set 1–2,000. With N = 1,000 and default parameters, 100 runs runs near-instantly. For presentations 500–1,000 runs give smoother distributions.

🚀 How to Run & Deploy

Run locally

pip install streamlit numpy pandas matplotlib streamlit run app.py

requirements.txt

streamlit numpy pandas matplotlib

Deploy to Streamlit Community Cloud (free, shareable URL)

  1. Push app.py and requirements.txt to a public GitHub repository.
  2. Go to share.streamlit.io and sign in with GitHub.
  3. Click New app → select your repo, branch, and app.py.
  4. Click Deploy. The app will be live at a public URL within ~2 minutes.
  5. Share the URL — anyone can open it in a browser, no Python installation needed.
